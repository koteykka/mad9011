## MAD9011 Final Project

This is a joint final project with your programming course. You design the site here, then you build it with Steve. Same project, two halves.
Design something you would actually want to code, because you are the person who has to code it. And design it properly, because vague design work turns into painful coding work.

## What you’re designing
Three pages, designed twice: once for desktop and once for mobile. Six screens in total, all in one Figma file.
Desktop frames are 1440 px wide and as tall as the page needs. Mobile frames are 393 px wide and 852 px tall, or taller if the page scrolls.
The mobile version is not the desktop version squeezed narrow. Things move, stack and change size. Your navigation in particular will almost certainly need to become a menu button on mobile.

## What every design must include:
## A favicon
The small icon that sits in the browser tab. Design it at 32 x 32 px and show it at actual size in your file, because that is the only way to know whether it works. A full logo almost never survives at that size. Usually one letter, one shape or one simplified piece of your mark.

## A header
The same header on all three pages, carrying your navigation bar. Navigation needs at least Home, Shop and Register. Show what a link looks like when you hover over it and which link is the current page.

## Page 1 — Home
A main banner at the top: a headline, a line of supporting text, and a button.

Under the banner, one featured item — a single card given room to itself, showing what the business is leading with. Image, name, price, a short line of description and a button.
Below it, content that animates as the user scrolls — elements appearing as they come into view. You are not building the animation in this course. You are writing down exactly what it should do, because you are the one who will code it afterwards in Steve’s course. Write it clearly enough that it still makes sense to you in three weeks. For each element that moves, write down what it is, what it does (fade in, slide up, scale), how long it takes, and in what order.

## Page 2 — Products and services

A list of cards, one per product or service. Show at least four.
Each card needs:

Product image
Product name
Price
Description
A Buy button


The Buy button is the main action on a card, so it should be the most prominent thing on it after the image. Show it in its normal, hover and pressed states.
These cards animate into view as the user scrolls. Say in your animation spec how they arrive — fading in, sliding up, one after another — and how long each takes.

## Page 3 — Purchase 
A purchase form, where the customer finishes the order.
At minimum: name, email, what they are buying, and a confirm button. Add whatever else your purchase actually needs — quantity, size, a date for a session, delivery details, payment fields. Design two versions of it: the form empty, and the form filled in.

## Choosing your idea
Pick something that sells — a product or a service. You need things to put on cards and something a customer can actually buy.
The list below mixes both. A service business works just as well as a shop, and sometimes better, because selling something you cannot photograph is a harder and more interesting design problem.

Products: crystals and minerals, houseplants, specialty coffee beans, handmade candles, sneakers, vinyl records, bike or skate parts, art prints and posters, skincare, tea and brewing kits, perfume, jewellery, camping and outdoor gear, guitars and music gear, vintage and secondhand clothing.

Services: energy healing sessions, meditation courses, breathwork retreats, sound baths and sound healing, astrology and tarot readings.

You can use your own idea instead. It just has to have things to list on cards and something a customer can buy.

Everybody messages me on Slack with the idea they have chosen, whether it is from this list or your own.
