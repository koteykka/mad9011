### Exercise 1 - Create UI Banner

In this exercise you'll use selection tools, layer masks and artboards to build a small ad campaign for a mock product launch. By the end you'll have three banner sizes that share one visual identity, and you'll have practised the compositing and file-organization habits we'll use all semester.

## Step 1 — Choose a theme

Pick one:

1. **Local coffee roaster** — a new seasonal blend.
2. **Bike shop** — spring tune-up special.
3. **Bookstore** — a staff-picks event or reading series.
4. **Houseplant shop** — a starter kit for beginners.
5. **Language app** — free trial promotion.
6. **Music festival** — lineup announcement.
7. **Meal kit service** — first-box discount.
8. **Skincare brand** — a new product launch.
9. **Pet supplies** — grooming service or subscription box.
10. **Travel agency** — a specific destination package.

Or propose your own. If you'd rather do something else, go ahead — just make sure it's a real-feeling product or service with something specific to advertise.

## Step 2 — Set up your artboards

Create one Photoshop document containing three artboards:

banner-horizontal	- 1200 × 628 px.
banner-square	- 1080 × 1080 px.
banner-vertical	- 1080 × 1920 px.

Name each artboard exactly as shown. Photoshop uses artboard names as filenames when you export, so getting this right now saves you renaming files later.

All three go in one document. Don't create three separate files.

## Step 3 — Gather and prepare your images

Find at least 3 images related to your theme — product shots, backgrounds, textures.
Use the Quick Selection, Object Selection or Magic Wand tool to isolate a key element, such as your product or model
Convert your selection to a layer mask rather than erasing the background


## Step 4 — Design the three banners

Each banner needs a headline, a short supporting line or offer, and a call to action (for example: Shop Now, Learn More, Get Started).

Across all three, keep the same colours, typefaces and imagery. It should read as one campaign.

Within each one, lay the content out for that shape. The vertical banner is not the horizontal one stretched — it needs its own arrangement. Think about where the eye lands first and make sure your text stays readable against whatever is behind it.

For the vertical format, keep important content out of the top ~250 px and bottom ~350 px. That's where platform interface elements sit on Stories and Reels.

## Step 5 — Organize your file

Before you export:

Every layer has a descriptive name.
Related layers are grouped.
Any element appearing on more than one artboard is a Smart Object.
Nothing has been erased that should have been masked.
Nothing is flattened or merged.

## Step 6 — Export and submit

Export all three artboards as JPEG using File → Export → Export As.

Package into a single .zip named lastname-firstname-exercise1 containing:

Your .psd working file, layers intact
Your three exported .jpeg files

### Exercise 2 - Campaign Analysis

In this exercise you'll pick a real campaign you admire, collect its different formats, and break down what makes it work.

## Step 1 — Choose a campaign
Pick a brand campaign that caught your attention — Apple, Nike, Mercedes, Rolex, Spotify, Airbnb, a local business, anything.

Where to find them: brand websites, their Instagram, Behance, Dribbble, Pinterest, or ad archives like Ads of the World and Meta Ad Library.

## Step 2 — Build your board

Collect the campaign images and arrange them on one artboard in Photoshop or Illustrator. Either program is fine.
1. **Lay them out** clearly so each format is easy to see.
2. **Keep it clean and organised.** This is a presentation board, so how you arrange it counts

Export as JPEG.

## Step 3 — Write your analysis

Answer these in 300–500 words. Write in your own words — this isn't a research report, it's your opinion backed up by what you can see.

## The campaign
What brand is this, and what are they advertising? Where did you find it?

## Typography
What fonts are used? Serif, sans serif, script, display? If there's more than one, how do they work together? Why do you think the brand chose these fonts? What do they say about the brand?

## Colour
What's the palette? What mood does it create, and does it match the product?

## Imagery and graphics
What kind of images are used — photography, illustration, product shots? How much space does the product take up? Are there any graphic elements — shapes, textures, patterns — and what do they add?

## Call to action
Is there one? How does it stand out — or does it? If there isn't one, why do you think they left it out?

## Your takeaway
What's the single strongest thing about this design? Name one thing you'd apply to your own work.
