Week Two - Photo Retouching Techniques

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, students will be able to:

  1. Make Selections: Isolate part of an image using Object Selection.
  2. Use Layer Masks: Hide parts of a layer without erasing them, and refine a mask by painting black and white.
  3. Clean Up a Photo: Remove distracting elements from a background using the Spot Healing Brush, Content-Aware Fill, and Generative Fill.
  4. Organize a Layered File: Group layers, apply layer styles, and lock layers to protect them.
  5. Use Smart Objects: Understand what a Smart Object is, why transforms stay lossless, and how one edit updates every copy.
  6. Adapt a Layout Across Formats: Reflow a design for horizontal, square, and vertical shapes while keeping one visual identity.
  7. Export a Campaign: Export all artboards at the correct sizes with proper file naming.

1. Making Selections

Section titled “1. Making Selections”

A selection isolates part of an image so you can work on that area without affecting the rest. Cutting a product out of its background is the most common single task in banner design.

The two you’ll use most:

Section titled “The two you’ll use most:”

Object Selection tool — hover over an element and Photoshop identifies it; click to select Select Subject — one command, isolates the main subject of the image

Other selection tools you’ll come across:

Quick Selection tool — paint roughly over an area and Photoshop expands the selection to match edges Magic Wand — selects areas of similar colour. Useful when your product sits on a flat, even background

2. Layer Masks

Section titled “2. Layer Masks”

A layer mask hides parts of a layer without erasing them. Nothing is deleted, so you can always bring the hidden pixels back.

To add a mask:

Make your selection. Click Add Layer Mask at the bottom of the Layers panel. Everything outside your selection is now hidden.

To refine it: paint on the mask with a soft brush. Black hides, white brings back.

The Eraser tool deletes pixels permanently. A layer mask hides them reversibly. In this course, use masks.

3. Cleaning Up a Photo

Section titled “3. Cleaning Up a Photo”

Sometimes a good product photo has one bad thing in it — a sign, a stray hand, a logo you can’t use. These tools handle almost every case. Spot Healing Brush

Click on a small blemish and Photoshop blends it with the surrounding pixels. No sampling needed. Best for small, isolated marks.

Content-Aware Fill

Section titled “Content-Aware Fill”

Make a selection, then Edit → Content-Aware Fill. Photoshop fills the area using surrounding content, and you can paint to control which areas it samples from.

In the Content-Aware Fill dialogue, set Output to: New Layer.

Generative Fill

Section titled “Generative Fill”

Select an area and describe what should be there. Best used for extending a background to fit an artboard the photo wasn’t shot for.

Where generative tools help and where they don’t They’re good for removing distractions and extending backgrounds. They’re unreliable for text, fine detail, and anything needing precision — they won’t match a brand colour or reproduce a logo correctly. And running the same prompt twice gives you two different results, which is a problem when your three banners need to match.

4. Layer Craft

Section titled “4. Layer Craft”

Your banner will end up with a dozen or more layers. These are the habits that keep it workable.

Grouping — select related layers and press Cmd/Ctrl + G. Name the group. A banner with groups for background, product, text and cleanup is readable at a glance.

Section titled “Grouping — select related layers and press Cmd/Ctrl + G. Name the group. A banner with groups for background, product, text and cleanup is readable at a glance.”

Layer styles — double-click a layer to open Layer Style. The ones you’ll use on banners:

Drop Shadow — lifts a headline off a busy photo Stroke — outlines a button or a cut-out product Gradient Overlay — adds depth to a button or a colour block

Locking — click the lock icon to protect a layer from accidental edits. Lock your background once it’s positioned and it will stop shifting every time you click.

5. Making Text Readable Over an Image

Section titled “5. Making Text Readable Over an Image”

The most common failure in banner design is a headline you can’t read. Three fixes, in order of preference:

1. Move the text. Find a quieter area of the photo. Cheapest solution and usually the best one.

2. Darken the area behind it. Add a black layer above the image, mask it to just the region under your text, and lower the opacity. A gradient along one edge works well and looks deliberate.

3. Adjust the image.* Click the New Adjustment Layer icon at the bottom of the Layers panel. Adjustments affect every layer beneath them — right-click and Create Clipping Mask to confine one to a single layer.

The two you’ll use most:

Brightness/Contrast — quick correction of a flat or overexposed photo

6. Smart Objects

Section titled “6. Smart Objects”

What is a Smart Object?

Section titled “What is a Smart Object?”

A normal layer is just pixels. When you scale it down, Photoshop throws away the pixels it no longer needs — and they’re gone for good.

A Smart Object is a container that holds the original image inside it. What you see on your canvas is a live preview of that original, not the original itself. Photoshop keeps the full-quality source tucked away, so every transform is applied to the preview rather than the actual pixels.

Two things follow from that.

Transforms stay lossless. Scale a Smart Object down, then back up, and it returns at full quality, because the original was never touched. Do that to a normal layer and the detail is gone permanently.

One edit updates every copy. Copies of a Smart Object all point back to the same source. Edit that source once and every copy updates. This is what keeps three banners consistent — change the product photo once instead of three times.

To create one: right-click a layer → Convert to Smart Object. Or place images with File → Place Embedded, which makes them Smart Objects automatically.

To edit one: double-click its thumbnail in the Layers panel. It opens in a new tab. Make your changes, save with Cmd/Ctrl + S, close the tab, and every copy updates.

7. Adapting Your Layout Across Formats

Section titled “7. Adapting Your Layout Across Formats”

Your three banners share a campaign. They do not share a layout.

Same: colours, typefaces, imagery, message. Different: where everything sits. Horizontal puts text beside the image, square balances them, vertical stacks them. Safe zones (vertical only). On Stories and Reels, platform buttons sit over your design. Keep your headline and call to action out of the top ~250 px and bottom ~350 px. Your background image can still fill the whole frame.

8. Exporting

Section titled “8. Exporting”

Export all artboards with File → Export → Export As.

Choose PNG for banners with crisp text and graphics. Select which artboards to include, or export all at once. Photoshop names files from your artboard names, so check those first.

Naming conventions

Section titled “Naming conventions”

1. Lowercase, no spaces — banner-square, not Banner Square 2. Hyphens rather than underscores 3. Descriptive, not positional — banner-vertical, not banner-3

Type basics

Section titled “Type basics”

1. Two typefaces maximum, or one with different weights 2. Make the size difference between headline and supporting text obvious — timid contrast reads as a mistake 3. Watch line spacing on big headlines, since the default is usually too loose
